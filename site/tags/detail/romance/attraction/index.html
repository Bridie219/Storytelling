01:51

This scene is a callback to the first ball Elizabeth and Mr Darcy both attended, where she asked if he danced and he said "not if I can help it." His decision to ask her to dance explores the attraction he feels for her in a very subtle way. It makes it clear that though he dislikes dancing, he would happily dance with her. His lack of expression could be interpreted as dismissive formality (which is likely how Elizabeth sees it) but it could also be interpreted as nerves. Elizabeth is completely shocked at this turn of events, because she is still acting under the assumption that he doesn't like her. He has not done the best job of showing her his interest. His decision to ask Elizabeth to dance is his announcement of his feelings and regard for her, but she doesn't pick up on that.

A Case Study of Romance: Pride and Prejudice - Elizabeth's Attraction

Romance
Attraction

00:00

Elizabeth is feeling rather out of sorts after Mr Darcy's proposal to her. It's almost like she's grieving the life she could had led, if Mr Darcy were a more tolerable version of himself. She also doesn't feel much like being out in society because she doesn't want to risk encountering Mr Darcy. She is also beginning to slowly realise he was right about everything he said, and she was quite harsh. He is about to dismantle the last argument she had, which was his dislike of Mr Wickham, through the letter.

Romance
Attraction

02:02

Mr Darcy's letter explaining his dislike of Mr Wickham is the final barrier removal between him and Elizabeth. His defence of his sister also endears him to Elizabeth, because it's the kind of thing she would do for her family. She starts to recognise that he might have a reason for his actions, and begins to search for those reasons, instead of making assumptions. His love for his sister is one of the things that kindles the start of Elizabeth's developing attraction to Mr Darcy.

Romance
Attraction

02:56

The second part of Mr Darcy's letter addresses his actions in preventing the union between Jane and Mr Bingley. He explains that he thought what he was doing was in defence of his friend. It assists Elizabeth in realising that Mr Darcy is steadfastly loyal to those he loves, which is a trait Jane also shares - even if they are both a little blind in their defence of their families.

A Case Study of Romance: Pride and Prejudice - The Barrier

Romance
Attraction

00:21

Mr Wickham picking up the handkerchief for Elizabeth initiates the start of their relationship, and acts as their initial "meeting". Mr Wickham makes a much better, more charming impression, than Mr Darcy. He acts as a barrier between the two because of his charm, but also because of his longstanding feud with Mr Darcy.

Romance
Attraction

00:46

Elizabeth is charmed by his demeanour and his self-deprecating nature. He acts as a kind of foil to Mr Darcy, who is closed off and prideful. Mr Wickham becomes the charming figure that Elizabeth is drawn to, further cementing his status as the barrier between Elizabeth and Mr Darcy. Later, it is because of Mr Wickham that Elizabeth realises her growing attraction to Mr Darcy, especially when he begins to help her family out of a problematic situation Mr Wickham put them in.
